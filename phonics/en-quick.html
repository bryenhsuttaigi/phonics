Tone sandhi — last syllable of a sense-unit keeps its citation tone; everything before it shifts

The rule in one line: "Lie low, make one point."

Tone 2 is outside the standard set — it is a special sandhi: in an emphatic tripled word, the first syllable goes to Rise (滑滑滑 kkut/).

Closing rimes -p / -t / -k / -m — no special tone rules, no special sandhi

How a syllable closes belongs to the rime, not to the tone. Closing-rime words take all seven tones and follow the same sandhi table as everything else. Two ordinary words, worked through:

WordSyllableToneSpelling
排骨 "pork ribs"骨 (final)Clip — citationkkut.

Two more fall out of the same rules: 排骨酥 "fried rib nuggets" kkut¯ (Clip→4→1, the shift run to its end) and 滑滑滑 kkut/ (special sandhi).

Zero-initial rule: an i glide is written y (ya, yahm, yong…), but the i in in and ing is the nucleus and stays. Nasal codas take the h (yahm, yahng); -p/-t/-k codas don't need it (yap, yok, ut).

Five sentences to say out loud

  • 你好 "hello" lee\-huh\｜多謝 "thank you" ttuh¯-sia+｜食飽未 "have you eaten?" tsia+-ppah\ bueh+-
  • 飲茶 "drink tea" lim¯-tteh~｜我是台灣人 "I am Taiwanese" gua\-see+-ttai~-uan¯-lahng~

Reading the hyphen. A trailing - means the syllable is written in its citation tone and shifts in running speech (one step per the sandhi table). No hyphen means the mark is already the spoken tone — say it exactly as written. Same rule in any notation.
